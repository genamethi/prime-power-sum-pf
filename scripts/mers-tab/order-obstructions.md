# An order obstruction for all N

Put r=ord_q(2), E=lcm(2,r), rho=v_q(2^r−1), a=(2^E−1)/q^rho. In the remaining case, a divides A, ord_a(q) divides N, and gcd(E,N)=1.

## Proven certificate

Let ℓ^f divide a, let s be a prime divisor of r, and write φ(ℓ^f)=s^v w with s not dividing w. If q^w is not 1 modulo ℓ^f, then s divides ord_(ℓ^f)(q). Consequently s divides both E and N, a contradiction. This excludes every N for that q.
Proof: ord_(ℓ^f)(q) divides s^v w. If its order were coprime to s, it would divide w, contrary to the displayed residue.

## Research question

Can the factorization of 2^E−1 force such a certificate? Equivalently, can one prove gcd(r,ord_a(q))>1, either uniformly under our remaining assumptions or outside an effectively bounded set? This is not proved here.
The experiment below first tests the stronger question for every odd prime q with E in the stated range, without imposing the q residue conditions or even rho. It distinguishes evidence for that wider question from the repeated-factor case required by the notes.

## Kummer formulation

For a prime-level certificate, let s divide r and ℓ−1, v=v_s(ℓ−1), w=(ℓ−1)/s^v, K=Q(ζ_(s^v)), and F=K(q^(1/s^v)). A prime P of K above ℓ has residue field F_ℓ. For α=q^(1/s^v), arithmetic Frobenius satisfies Frob_P(α)/α = q^w modulo a prime of F above P. Hence q^w≠1 detects nontrivial Frobenius in F/K and forces s to divide ord_ℓ(q). See [Stevenhagen, Kummer Theory and Reciprocity Laws](https://websites.math.leidenuniv.nl/algebra/Stevenhagen-Kummer.pdf), Sections 2 and 4.
The task is to force this nontrivial Frobenius at a prime dividing the specific integer a. If the witness only appears modulo ℓ^f with f>1 and s=ℓ, use the prime-power order test above; the prime-level Kummer formulation does not cover that case.
